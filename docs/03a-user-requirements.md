# VoidScope user requirements

What the crews, medical leads and trainers who would use VoidScope need, written from their side. The product requirements that meet these needs are in VDS-PRS-001 (`03-requirements.md`) and the links are in the traceability matrix (`03b-traceability.md`). Method: STANDARDS section 22.

*Table 1. User requirements.*

| ID | Category | Title | Need | Priority | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| UR-001 | Performance | Fits the hole we can make | The probe goes through a standard 50 mm (2 in) core hole or gap without enlarging it | High | Municipal fire and rescue crews (PRB Table 1) |
| UR-002 | Performance | Reaches into the void | The probe reaches at least 4 m along a straight path into a void | High | Municipal fire and rescue crews; USAR trainers |
| UR-003 | Performance | Sees in darkness and dust | The operator can read a letter or recognise a person in a dark, dusty void on the monitor | High | Municipal fire and rescue crews; volunteer teams |
| UR-004 | Environment and durability | Survives rubble | The head and rod survive handling, drops, dust, mud and water on a collapse site | High | Volunteer and community emergency response teams |
| UR-005 | Safety | Water cannot harm the survivor | Water reaches a survivor only in small, controlled amounts that the survivor takes by choice, under the medical lead's direction | High | Medical lead on a rescue team |
| UR-006 | Safety | Air is clean and gentle | Air reaches the void clean and at a pressure that cannot hurt a survivor or disturb the debris | High | Medical lead on a rescue team |
| UR-007 | Environment and durability | Works a whole shift | The kit runs a full shift on battery in cold and in heat, with no mains power | High | Volunteer and community emergency response teams |
| UR-008 | Cost | Affordable for a municipal budget | The full kit costs a small fraction of a commercial search camera | High | Municipal fire and rescue crews; volunteer teams |
| UR-009 | Deployment and assembly | Quick to set up and carry | Two trained people carry it and are searching within minutes of arrival | Medium | Municipal fire and rescue crews |
| UR-010 | Maintenance | Water path stays clean | The parts that touch water are cheap and quick to replace between survivors | Medium | Medical lead on a rescue team |
| UR-011 | Performance | Looks round a corner | The operator can look down a passage that turns away from a straight hole | High | Municipal fire and rescue crews (Amish, decision 3A, 2026-10-03) |
| UR-012 | Usability | Talks with the survivor | The operator can speak with and hear a survivor at the tip | Medium | Medical lead on a rescue team |
| UR-013 | Safety | Nothing hot or hazardous at the tip | The part that goes near a person is not hot enough to burn, and the battery is not a hazard | Medium | Medical lead on a rescue team |
| UR-014 | Maintenance | Repairable with commodity parts | A team can repair the kit with common stock, hand tools and a basic 3D printer | Medium | Volunteer and community emergency response teams |
| UR-015 | Usability | Good for drills | Trainers can use the same probe repeatedly in drills with a written procedure | Low | USAR trainers |
| UR-016 | Regulatory | Honest about its status | The design is published as an open engineering reference, not as certified rescue or medical equipment | Low | Stakeholders (PRB Table 2) |

Categories: deployment and assembly, performance, usability, safety, environment and durability, logistics, maintenance, cost, regulatory. Priorities: High, Medium, Low.
